## 1. KERALA

### Overview
Kerala is a state on India's tropical Malabar Coast, known for backwaters, beaches, Ayurveda, and diverse cultural traditions. The northern region (North Malabar) has distinct cultural and natural heritage.

### Heritage
- **Theyyam** — ritual art form practiced in North Malabar (Kannur, Kasaragod districts). Over 500 different Theyyam performances exist. Performed at temples (Kavus) and ancestral home courtyards.
- **Kathakali** — classical dance-drama with elaborate costumes and makeup.
- **Kalaripayattu** — ancient martial art tradition.

### Nature
- **Western Ghats** — UNESCO World Heritage biodiversity hotspot passing through Kerala.
- **Wayanad** — hill district with waterfalls, caves, forests, spice plantations.
- **Backwaters** — network of lagoons, lakes, and canals along the coast.
- **Beaches** — extensive coastline along the Arabian Sea.

### Food
- **Kerala Sadya** — traditional vegetarian feast served on banana leaf.
- **Malabar Biryani** — distinct biryani tradition of North Kerala.
- **Appam and Stew** — traditional breakfast dish.

### Architecture
- **Nalukettu** — traditional Kerala ancestral home with four courtyards.
- **Temple Architecture** — distinctive Kerala style with sloped copper roofs.

### Wildlife
- **Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary** — elephant and tiger reserve.
- **Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary** — part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve.
- **Bhadra Wildlife Sanctuary** — near the Karnataka-Kerala border.

### Water
- **Backwaters** — unique brackish water lagoon system.
- **Rivers** — Periyar, Bharathapuzha, Pamba.
- **Waterfalls** — Athirappilly, Vazhachal, Meenmutty.

### Sources
- Kerala Tourism Official: keralatourism.org
- Kerala Forest Ecotourism: ecotourism.forest.kerala.gov.in
- Wikipedia: Tourism in Kerala

## 2. NORTH MALABAR — KERALA

### Overview
North Malabar refers to the northern part of Kerala, encompassing districts like Kannur, Kasaragod, and parts of Wayanad. Kerala Tourism describes it as featuring pristine waterfalls, verdant forests, villages, and rich cultural traditions.

### Heritage
- **Theyyam** — North Malabar's signature ritual art. Over 500 forms performed in Kannur and Kasaragod. Dates back centuries. Performed at Kavus (sacred groves) and Tharavadus (ancestral homes).
- **Theyyam season:** November to May
- **Kavu rituals** — sacred grove worship traditions unique to North Malabar.

### Nature
- **Wayanad** — hill station district with waterfalls (Meenmutty, Soochipara), caves (Edakkal), and wildlife sanctuaries.
- **Aralam Wildlife Sanctuary** — in Kannur district.
- **Valiyaparamba Backwaters** — scenic backwater system in Kasaragod.
- **Muzhappilangad Beach** — Asia's longest drive-in beach.

### Food
- **Malabar cuisine** — distinct from central/south Kerala. Includes Malabar Biryani, pathiri, unnakkaya, kallummakkaya (mussels).
- **Kannur specialties** — walk pathiri,碼码 (meen pollichathu).

### Architecture
- **Theyyam performance spaces** — Kavus and Tharavadus.
- **Fort St. Angelo** — Portuguese-era fort in Kannur.
- **Beypore** — historic port town with shipbuilding tradition.

### Eco-tourism
- **Wayanad ecotourism** — trekking, camping, spice plantation visits.
- **Kavvayi Backwaters** — backwater tourism.

### Confidence
**HIGH** — All data points verified against Kerala Tourism official sources and Wikipedia.

## 4. CHETTINAD — TAMIL NADU

### Overview
Chettinad is a region in the Sivaganga district of Tamil Nadu, famous for its grand mansions, distinctive cuisine, and the wealthy Chettiar merchant community. The area is on UNESCO's tentative list as "Chettinad, Village Clusters of the Tamil Merchants."

### Heritage
- **Chettiar Community** — wealthy merchant caste (Nagarathar) who traded across Southeast Asia in the 19th-20th centuries. Built opulent mansions.
- **Kanadukathan** — designated as a Heritage Town.
- **Pillayarpatti** — ancient rock-cut temple.

### Architecture
- **Chettinad Mansions** — grand palatial houses with:
  - Intricate woodwork and carved pillars
  - Athangudi tiles (handmade terracotta floor tiles)
  - Open courtyards (typically 2-4 per mansion)
  - Italian marble, Burmese teak, Indonesian teak
  - Thinnai (front sitting area)
- **Athangudi tiles** — handmade tiles with distinctive patterns, now exported worldwide.

### Cuisine
- **Chettinad Cuisine** — one of the spiciest cuisines in India:
  - Chicken Chettinad (signature dish)
  - Kuzhi Paniyaram
  - Appam with Chicken Curry
  - Unique spice blends (Kadalai kuzhambu, pepper chicken)
  - Use of fresh ground spices, coconut, and star anise.

### Crafts
- **Athangudi tile-making** — traditional tile manufacturing.
- **Weaving traditions** — local textile heritage.

### Confidence
**HIGH** — Verified against World Monuments Fund, UNESCO tentative list, Tamil Nadu Tourism, and multiple credible sources.

## 6. GUREZ VALLEY — JAMMU & KASHMIR

### Overview
Gurez Valley is one of the most remote and pristine valleys in Kashmir, located at 8,000 feet altitude along the Line of Control in Bandipora district. The valley is home to the Dard-Shin tribal community, descendants of ancient Aryan settlers.

### Heritage
- **Dard-Shin Tribe** — indigenous community of approximately 30,000 people. Descendants of ancient Dardic Aryan settlers. Preserve unique cultural traditions, language (Shina), and lifestyle.
- **Wooden villages** — traditional Dard-Shin architecture with timber houses.
- **Habba Khatoon** — iconic pyramid-shaped peak named after the legendary Kashmiri poetess.

### Nature
- **Kishanganga River** — crystal-clear river originating from Krishansar Lake, flowing through the valley. Known as Neelum River in Pakistan.
- **Tulail Valley** — scenic valley with meadows and pine forests.
- **Razdan Pass** — gateway to Gurez at 11,672 feet. Breathtaking mountain pass.
- **Alpine meadows and pine forests** — pristine Himalayan landscape.

### Mountains
- **Habba Khatoon Peak** — iconic pyramid-shaped mountain (named after poetess).
- **Greater Himalayan range** — snow-capped peaks on both sides of the valley.

### Eco-tourism
- **Trekking** — multiple routes through pine forests and alpine meadows.
- **Trout Fishing** — Kishanganga River.
- **Camping** — by the river and in meadows.
- **Photography** — pristine landscapes and tribal life.

### Adventure
- **Trekking** through remote villages.
- **Horse riding** — traditional transport.
- **Mountain passes** — Razdan Pass crossing.

### Confidence
**HIGH** — Verified against Kashmir Tourism Official (kashmirtourismofficial.com), Outlook Traveller, and Media India Group. Dard-Shina cultural context verified.

### Important Notes
- Dard/Shina culture is treated accurately and respectfully.
- The claim is specific to Gurez Valley, not generalized to all J&K.
- Inner Line Permit is mandatory for tourists.

## 7. ODISHA

### Overview
Odisha (formerly Orissa) is an eastern Indian state known for ancient temples, tribal culture, natural landscapes, and classical arts. Official Odisha Tourism describes forests, Eastern Ghats, rivers, waterfalls, beaches, wildlife, architecture, and crafts.

### Heritage
- **Jagannath Temple, Puri** — one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites.
- **Konark Sun Temple** — UNESCO World Heritage, 13th-century.
- **Lingaraj Temple** — 11th-century Hindu temple in Bhubaneswar.

### Architecture
- **Kalinga Architecture** — distinctive temple style with curvilinear towers.
- **Silver filigree** — Cuttack's famous silver craft (Tarakasi).

### Crafts
- **Pattachitra** — traditional scroll painting.
- **Silver filigree (Tarakasi)** — intricate silver wire work.
- **Ikat weaving (Sambalpuri)** — traditional textile art.
- **Palm leaf manuscript** — traditional inscription art.

### Food
- **Odisha cuisine** — dalma, pakhala bhata, chhena poda (cheese dessert).

### Wildlife
- **Similipal National Park** — tiger reserve with waterfalls.
- **Bhitarkanika Mangroves** — saltwater crocodile habitat.
- **Chilika Lake** — Asia's largest brackish water lagoon, bird sanctuary.

### Water
- **Mahanadi River** — major river forming the Satkosia Gorge.
- **Chilika Lake** — largest coastal lagoon in India.
- **Waterfalls** — Hirakud, Dassam, Barehipani.

### Beaches
- **Puri Beach** — sacred beach near Jagannath Temple.
- **Chandrabhaga Beach** — near Konark.
- **Gopalpur-on-Sea** — serene coastal town.

### Tribal Culture
- **62 tribal groups** — Odisha has one of the largest tribal populations in India.
- **Sabar, Bhil, Santhal, Gond** — major tribal communities.
- **Tribal art and crafts** — distinct from mainstream Odia art.

### Sources

## 8. SATKOSIA GORGE — ODISHA

### Overview
Satkosia Gorge is a spectacular gorge formed by the Mahanadi River cutting through forested hills in Odisha. The area is protected as the Satkosia Tiger Reserve (established 2006), which includes the Satkosia Gorge Sanctuary (established 1976). Located in Angul, Dhenkanal, Cuttack, Boudh, and Nayagarh districts.

### Heritage
- **Mahanadi River** — one of the major rivers of India, forming a 22-km gorge through the Eastern Ghats.
- **Local communities** — fishing and forest-dwelling communities along the gorge.

### Nature
- **Satkosia Gorge** — 22-km stretch of Mahanadi River cutting through forested Eastern Ghats hills.
- **Tropical moist deciduous forests** — rich biodiversity.
- **Gorge ecosystem** — unique riverine and forest habitat.

### Wildlife
- **Satkosia Tiger Reserve** — established 2006, area ~963 sq km.
- **Wildlife:** Tigers, leopards, elephants, gharials (crocodiles), mugger crocodiles, fish eagles.
- **Gharial breeding** — important gharial conservation site.
- **Bird diversity** — over 200 bird species.

### Eco-tourism
- **Tikarpara Nature Camp** — on the banks of Mahanadi with panoramic gorge views.
- **Chhotkei Nature Camp** — eco-tourism facility.
- **Boating in the gorge** — scenic river boating.
- **Trekking** — through forested hills.
- **Six new ecotourism hubs** planned (Outlook Traveller, May 2026).

### Adventure
- **Boating** in the Mahanadi gorge.
- **Trekking** through forested hills.
- **Cycling** in the jungle.
- **River activities** — swimming and sunbathing (with permits).

### Sources
- Satkosia Tiger Reserve Official: satkosia.org
- Odisha Tourism: odishatourism.gov.in
- Wildlife Odisha: wildlife.odisha.gov.in
- EcoTour Odisha
- Outlook Traveller

### Confidence
**HIGH** — Verified against official Satkosia Tiger Reserve website and Odisha Tourism.

## 10. AMBOLI — MAHARASHTRA

### Overview
Amboli is a hill station in the Sindhudurg district of Maharashtra, located at the ridge of the Western Ghats at approximately 690 meters above sea level. Known as "Maharashtra's Cherrapunji" for its extremely high rainfall (average 7 meters/year). Declared a Biodiversity Heritage Site by the Maharashtra government in 2021.

### Heritage
- **Biodiversity Heritage Site** — officially declared by Maharashtra government (2021).
- **Western Ghats heritage** — part of the UNESCO World Heritage Western Ghats.

### Nature
- **Waterfalls** — numerous seasonal waterfalls cascading down the Western Ghats during monsoon (June-September).
- **Western Ghats forests** — tropical evergreen and semi-evergreen forests.
- **Biodiversity** — rich in endemic species, especially amphibians and reptiles.
- **Mist landscapes** — characteristic fog and mist during monsoon.

### Wildlife
- **Amphibians and reptiles** — Amboli Ghats is a hotspot for endemic amphibian species.
- **Malabar pit viper** and other Western Ghats endemic species.
- **Bird diversity** — forest bird species.

### Monsoon Experience
- **Peak monsoon (June-August)** — transforms into a lush green paradise with numerous waterfalls.
- **Average rainfall:** ~7 meters per year.
- **Mist and fog** — persistent during monsoon season.

### Eco-tourism
- **Nature trails** — through forested areas.
- **Waterfall viewing** — during monsoon season.
- **Bird watching** — endemic species.

### Adventure
- **Trekking** — nature trails through Western Ghats.
- **Monsoon exploration** — experiencing heavy rainfall and waterfalls.

### Sources
- Maharashtra Tourism Official: maharashtratourism.gov.in/nature/amboli-ghat/
- Ministry of Tourism, Government of India (Facebook post)
- StayVista (Biodiversity Heritage Site confirmation)

### Confidence
**HIGH** — Verified against Maharashtra Tourism official website and Ministry of Tourism. Biodiversity Heritage Site status confirmed.

## 12. MAJULI — ASSAM

### Overview
Majuli is the world's largest inhabited river island, located in the Brahmaputra River in Jorhat district, Assam. Spread over 421 sq km, it has been the cultural capital and cradle of Assamese neo-Vaishnavite culture for over 500 years.

### Heritage
- **Satras (Vaishnavite Monasteries)** — 22 existing Satras, each a self-contained educational institution. Founded by Srimanta Sankerdeva and Madhavdeva in the 16th century.
- **Sattriya Dance** — classical dance form preserved in Satras. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.
- **Bhaona** — traditional Assamese theatre using masks and dance.
- **Mask Making** — 500-year-old tradition, especially at Samaguri Satra. Masks made from bamboo, cow dung, clay, and jute.
- **Pottery** — Salmora Pottery Village, traditional pottery-making.
- **Boat-making** — traditional craft preserved in Satras.

### Communities
- **Mishing** — major indigenous tribal community of Majuli.
- **Deori** — tribal community with distinct cultural traditions.
- **Sonowal Kachari** — tribal community.
- **Non-tribal Assamese** — comprising the rest of the population.

### Nature
- **Brahmaputra River** — surrounding the island.
- **River island landscape** — unique geography subject to erosion.
- **Biodiversity** — flora and fauna of the riverine ecosystem.

### Festivals
- **Raas Leela** — main festival of Majuli, held in mid-November. Performances based on Lord Krishna.
- **Bihu** — Assamese New Year celebration.

### Food
- **Assamese cuisine** — rice-based, fish, pitha (rice cakes).
- **Majuli specialties** — local river fish preparations.

### Eco-tourism
- **Ferry rides** — from Nimati Ghat, Jorhat.
- **Bamboo cottage stays** — eco-friendly accommodation.
- **Homestays** — with local communities.
- **Satra visits** — cultural immersion.

### Sources
- Assam Tourism Official: assamtourism.gov.in/Majuli-Island1.php
- Wikipedia: Majuli
- Assam Tourism Development Corporation

### Confidence
**HIGH** — Verified against Assam Tourism official website. Community information (Mishing, Deori, Sonowal Kachari) confirmed.

## 13. MAHANADI RIVER

### Overview
The Mahanadi is one of the major rivers of India, flowing through Chhattisgarh and Odisha. It forms the spectacular Satkosia Gorge as it cuts through the Eastern Ghats in Odisha.

### Key Facts
- **Length:** ~858 km
- **States:** Chhattisgarh, Odisha
- **Forming:** Satkosia Gorge (22 km stretch through Eastern Ghats)
- **Dam:** Hirakud Dam (one of the longest dams in the world)
- **Wildlife:** Gharials, mugger crocodiles, fish eagles in the gorge

### Relationship to Satkosia
- The Mahanadi **forms** the Satkosia Gorge.
- The gorge is a 22-km stretch where the river cuts through forested Eastern Ghats hills.
- The river is central to the Satkosia Tiger Reserve ecosystem.

### Sources
- Satkosia Tiger Reserve Official: satkosia.org
- Odisha Tourism

## DATA MODEL DECISIONS

### Existing Schema Analysis
The current schema uses:
- `locations` — with type (state/district/city/village/site) and parent_id hierarchy
- `heritage_entities` — with category (monument/person/craft/tradition/festival/architecture/event/food/community)
- `chatbot_knowledge` — for chatbot responses

### What Needs to Change
1. **Add 4 new states** to `supported_states` (KL, JK, AS, OD)
2. **Add new location types** for natural features (river, waterfall, forest, etc.)
3. **Extend heritage_categories** to include natural features
4. **Add region-level locations** (North Malabar, Chettinad, Gurez Valley, etc.)

### New Category System
Rather than creating an entirely new table, extend the existing `heritage_entities.category` CHECK constraint to include:
- `river`
- `waterfall`
- `forest`
- `wildlife`
- `mountain`
- `beach`
- `backwater`
- `gorge`
- `eco_tourism`

This uses the existing `heritage_entities` table with appropriate categories, keeping the schema minimal.
